**Residues of the round‑35 change that did not reach every site:**

**F1 (MEDIUM — SPEC/client contract, R0/R5). The C5 limit entry misstates the sweep helper's capability.**
SPEC "Known limits", the round‑35 C5 bullet: *"The sweep helper can move the token out (the PDA is the owner) but cannot close the account."* The helper (`programs/cbob-vault-sweep/src/lib.rs`, `process`) transfers **and then unconditionally closes** each named pair in one invocation. SPL Token's `CloseAccount` requires the close authority; with a stranger's close authority set, the close CPI fails and **the whole invocation reverts, including the transfer**. The helper cannot move the token out of such an account at all. Round 35's own triage said this correctly ("named in the sweep it reverts at close"); the SPEC write‑up then garbled it. What an accident gains: a page builder following the SPEC's client contract ("Sweep through the helper… naming every reserve account") builds a redemption that always reverts for a C5‑afflicted vault — a holder‑facing lockout until the (mutable) page is fixed; nothing is lost. Evidence if I am right: on a validator, create a classic token account, `SetAuthority(CloseAccount, stranger)`, `SetAuthority(AccountOwner, vault PDA)`, fund one unit, call the helper with the pair — the invocation reverts at the close. Fix: rewrite the sentence ("the helper cannot be used for such an account; its close reverts the whole invocation") and add to item 50's client contract: move such a balance with a **direct Core `ExecuteV1` Token transfer**, leaving the account undeclared. The round‑35 web e2e record ("a full redemption including the C5 account") suggests the staged page already does something like this — the contract text should say what it does.

## R1 — The crank

I could not construct the four failure shapes asked for, and I looked hard:

- **A plan the program refuses:** the race codes are covered (`BENIGN` + `judgeDistributeRefusal` re‑reading the config; `PoolShort` never benign; `NothingToSplit` benign). The plan refuses to distribute with an unreadable eligible vault, a frozen/absent pool, unpaid‑for reveals (budgeted, not all‑or‑nothing), a split with unreadable decimals, and a split into a frozen/absent pool or Squads ATA (each named as a problem). The one soft spot I found is C11 (already known, INFO): `planCrank` hard‑codes `1500n` instead of reading `cfg.split_bps` — the two cannot diverge (written once at initialize), and the arithmetic is byte‑identical to the program's `bank_cut`/`holders`.
- **Pays or joins a dead vault:** no. `retireBurned` runs first; positively‑burned assets are excluded from preJoins, joins, activate and claims; `'other'` (unreadable) assets are excluded and reported; `retiringWeight`/`retiringOwed`/`retiringThisEpoch` plan against the retirements (round 22 F5 / 24 I1), and the level‑0 guard (`r.active`) keeps `wOf` from ever seeing `LEVEL_WEIGHTS[-1]`.
- **Out‑of‑order reveals:** no. The reveal loop runs strictly from `revealed_total`, breaks on the first failure, benign refusal, skip, or hash mismatch, and the hash is checked before the fee is spent (`judgeRevealAttrs`, with the `level`‑appended "revealed elsewhere" case).
- **Value anywhere unpinned:** no. Every instruction is a MINT instruction with program‑pinned destinations; the selftest asserts no transfer/System value movement and no non‑MINT program; the fee key signs only as payer; the reveal rent is budgeted with the floor kept in reserve (round 30 L2, both ways armed).

The boost arithmetic in the plan is the program's: pre‑expiry owed = `atExpiry + w·Δacc` (matches `reweigh`'s carry/debt restart), joined‑now owed = `w·Δacc` (matches `join`'s `debt = w·acc`), preJoins before the distribution, postExpires for `boost_end_epoch == dist_epoch+1` right after it, and the denominator `total_active_weight + preJoinWeight − retiringWeight − expiringWeight` matches the state the pass produces. ## R5 — The swap and the revenue cycle

**(a) Oracle math.** The exponent handling is correct for every sign (`e = sol.expo − btc.expo`; `e ≥ 0` scales the numerator, `e < 0` the denominator; both reduce to `lamports·sol/(btc·10)` at the live `−8/−8`); all arithmetic is BigInt (no overflow; `10n**BigInt(e)` is only reachable with a Pyth‑compromised exponent, which is accepted limit 10(d)); both divisions floor, which is the conservative direction for a minimum. The floor is genuinely below an honest pool return: 150 bps covers Orca's 16 bps fee, the impact of a 10‑SOL swap in a $10M pool, and the ≤30 bps confidence bound, with room.

**(b) Pyth verification.** The offsets (tag at 40, feed 41, price 73, conf 81, expo 89, publish_time 93) match `PriceUpdateV2` and the selftest's fixtures. Ownership by the receiver plus the derived sponsored address is sufficient **within the accepted third‑party trust**: only the receiver program can write the account, and it writes only Wormhole‑verified messages — a chosen price or a forged old `publish_time` requires compromising Pyth or the receiver (limit 10(d), watched at HIGH). The clock is the chain's Clock sysvar, cross‑checked for owner and length, with the 30 s future bound — not the box's.

**(c) Deviation filter.** `orcaSpot` = (sqrt_price/2⁶⁴)² is cbBTC sats per wSOL lamport (token B per token A); `meteoraSpot` = 1/(1+bs)^id is the inverse of lamports‑per‑sat — both in sats/lamport, both the right direction, both proven against the real pools by the fork (SW.0). Floating point cannot pass a pool it should refuse except at a ~1e‑7 bps boundary — immaterial against the 150 bps floor.

**(d) Minimum out.** Same oracle read as the deviation filter (one `oracleFloor` call per `market()`), different role by design (read‑time gate vs on‑chain floor); units are cbBTC base units (sats) in both `other_amount_threshold` and `min_amount_out`. Correct.

**(e) The judge.** It checks the output account (key 5 = the operator's own cbBTC ATA), the minimum‑out bytes (offset 16), the amount (offset 8), Orca's direction and price limit, the exact seven‑instruction shape with CU limit 400k and no price instruction (any extra instruction breaks the count; any `setComputeUnitPrice` is named), and both ATA creations key‑for‑key. What a tampered builder can still route past it: nothing that moves value — the unchecked venue accounts (vaults, tick/bin arrays, oracles, bitmap, reserves) are all validated by the venue programs, and a wrong account fails the swap with nothing moved. The residual is the accepted same‑tree tamper (limit 10(c)), backstopped by the post‑swap delta check.

**(f) Account derivation.** Tick arrays (floor semantics for negatives, current + two below for a_to_b) and bin arrays (active + two above for Y→X) match the published interfaces and are proven by the fork against live state; at a boundary the array containing the current tick is chosen; an uninitialised array fails the swap on chain (loud, nothing moves).

**(g) wSOL.** Wrap exactly `lamportsIn`, sync, exact‑input swap, close. A pre‑existing balance: `SyncNative` syncs the whole lamport balance, the swap consumes exactly the input, and `CloseAccount` on a native account returns **all** remaining lamports to the operator — nothing is stranded. wSOL has no freeze authority; a delegate does not block (the swap uses the owner as authority; close ignores delegates); the ATA address is program‑derived so it cannot be "created by someone else" for the operator. A failed swap reverts the whole transaction, so no input is left wrapped.

**(h) Failure and retry.** Orca first; Meteora only on a proven refusal (`classifySendFailure`: preflight refusal, landed‑and‑failed, or expired‑unlanded); an **unknown** outcome throws and never tries the second venue, so a confirmation timeout on a swap that later lands cannot double‑swap. Every path leaves the ledger open until a return is proven: `sendRejected` only on a proven refusal; the recovery closes a cycle only on chain evidence (`closesCycle` naming a real on‑chain split, or a provably‑empty revenue ATA); the open‑cycle guard blocks the next run. One conservative residue: a transport‑level send failure with no signature is classified `unknown` (correctly — the RPC may have accepted the transaction), which wedges the cycle open for hand recovery even when nothing moved; loud, documented, rare.

**(i) The bound.** Per cycle: ≤ 150 bps of the oracle‑derived expected output — ≤ 0.15 SOL on a 10‑SOL cycle. Per day at the ruled cadence: ≤ 0.6 SOL. Per 30‑day month: ≤ ~18 SOL, i.e. ~1.5 % of everything swept — about 0.15 % of resale volume at a 10 % royalty. A pool pushed to the gate edge adds nothing (the gate is read‑time; the floor binds execution); a stale‑but‑accepted oracle adds the market's true drift over ≤180 s on top, which the code does not bound and limit 10 says so in those words; a manipulated Meteora pool is bounded by the same floor (its thinner book only makes the swap fail). Material? To royalty recipients, real but small, and disclosed ("an honest swap can come in up to 1.5 % below Pyth's price"). **Operator key worst case:** unchanged in kind — 10 SOL per 6 h window until the guardian stops it; detection is now fast and flood‑immune (`sweep_unledgered_state` from account state within ~30 min), so the realistic exposure before a human acts is a few windows; unbounded only if the guardian is unreachable (limit 10's stated bound). The ledger cannot read a failed cycle as closed (verified above).

## GIVEN THAT NO HUMAN FIRM WILL EVER SEE THIS AND THE PROGRAMS WILL BE IMMUTABLE, what should this project do before launch that AI audit structurally cannot?

1. **Run the dress rehearsal (item 22) with a human at the terminal.** No reading of this tree can validate the crank's I/O at 2,100 vaults, the every‑minute reveal lane, RPC rate limits, or the revenue cycle against a live cluster. The named pass/fail items (including one full cycle through each pool and one forced post‑swap failure walked through the hand recovery) exist precisely because AI rounds cannot execute them. 2. **Give `web/` a focused quorate round before the freeze (F9), and close the freeze‑perimeter hole.** The code holders sign is the last thing being audited and the only part that can still change after the freeze without resetting the counter.
3. **A human verifies the four `initialize` transactions against `launch/mainnet-init.json` field‑by‑field, on screen, before signing.** The checker validates the JSON, not the transactions (round 24 A6) — and every value is permanent.
4. **Restore‑test the key backups instead of trusting include lists.** The deploy‑authority key's custody record says "in the nightly encrypted offsite backup… verified in snapshot `e5dce8ca`" — re‑verify at the release commit by actually restoring the file. Same for the $CBOB release key and the operator key. No code can check a backup.
5. **Rehearse the guardian's stops and the hand‑recovery runbook by doing them once.** `emergency_stop_sweep`, the open‑cycle recovery, and the Squads return have never been executed by a human; the runbook is prose until then.
6. **Budget the freeze rounds before starting them.** Round 35 lost quorum to `in_flight_budget_exhausted` (9 of 16 seats). The ship gate needs two consecutive clean quorate rounds with different rosters — fund the account, and have a fallback provider, or the freeze stalls for reasons no gate models.
7. **Write the third‑party‑upgrade response runbook.** The alarm detects Core/Orca/Meteora/Pyth changes; nothing decides what a human does in the first hour (pause sales? communicate? assess the vault‑opening path?). Write it before launch, not after the first page.
8. **A second human reads HOLDER‑GUIDE.md against the code, sentence by sentence, before publication.** Round 24's confirmed falsehoods were all there, and the guide is the buyer‑facing contract; the rescue and C5 passages (F1, F3) are the current weak spots.
