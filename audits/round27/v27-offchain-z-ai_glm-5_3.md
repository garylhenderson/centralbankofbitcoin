## R5 — the swap and the revenue cycle

- **(a) Oracle math.** Exponent handling is sign-correct for both feeds at −8 and for any sign combination (`e≥0` scales num, else den); all BigInt, floor rounding on both divisions (conservative); `satsPerLamport` is float but only feeds the deviation gate, where both prices are within Number's exact-integer range and the error is ~15 orders below the 100 bps threshold. The floor is genuinely below an honest pool return: 150 bps covers Orca's 16 bps fee plus measured impact (the fork's 10 SOL runs landed 250–420 bps *above* the floor).
- **(b) Pyth.** Offsets recomputed: disc 8, write_authority 32, verification tag at 40 (must be 1), feed id 41–73, price 73, conf 81, expo 89, publish_time 93 — consistent with the selftest fixture, the fork's `stamped` rewriter, and the fork's success against *real cloned* Pyth accounts. Ownership + derived address + full verification + expected feed + age/confidence/future bounds: a stranger cannot write the account (only the receiver program owns it, and the address is a PDA of the push oracle), and a malicious *Pyth upgrade* is the disclosed third-party trust (watched at HIGH). The age is judged on the Clock sysvar, owner-checked in `market()`; an unreadable Clock refuses.
- **(c) Deviation filter.** `orcaSpot` = (sqrt/2^64)² in raw B-per-A units = sats per lamport ✓; `meteoraSpot` = inverse bin power with X=cbBTC, Y=wSOL ✓ (reversed from Orca, as commented). Float cannot pass a pool it should refuse: the gate is ≤100 bps against a float whose error is ~1e-15 relative; the L6 arms drive 99/101 bps both sides through the real `market()`.
- **(d) Minimum out.** Same `minSats` object feeds both the deviation gate's oracle and the on-chain threshold; Orca `other_amount_threshold` and Meteora `min_amount_out` are both u64 at data offset 16 — asserted by the M1 arm and proven by the fork's 5%-high refusal on both venues.
- **(e) The judge.** It checks the exact seven-instruction sequence, both ATA creations key-for-key, the wrap amount, SyncNative, the swap's program/pool/input/output/amount/threshold/direction/price-limit/signer-slot, the single close, no second signer anywhere, no priority fee, and Token instructions limited to SyncNative+Close. A tampered builder's remaining freedom is the pool-owned accounts (bitmap, tick/bin arrays, vaults) and the `host_fee_in` None slot — both validated by the venue programs per their published interfaces, and both inside the disclosed box-owner residue (a tampered module can edit the judge). The post-signing delta check (`delta < guaranteed` throws, cycle open) is the second net. I could not construct a route past it that a same-tree tamper could not also simply remove.
- **(f) Account derivation.** Tick arrays (current, then two below, for a_to_b) and bin arrays (active, then two above, for Y→X) match the published interfaces and are proven by the fork against real cloned arrays; an uninitialised array at a boundary is a program refusal → cycle open, venue fallback only on refusal. The round-26 "unallocated sparse array" scenario remains unexercised and correctly stays listed as missing evidence, not disproved.
- **(g) wSOL.** Exactly `lamportsIn` wrapped, synced, closed back; a pre-existing balance returns with the close and is excluded from the delta by `opBefore`; a frozen or delegated account fails the swap loudly. Nothing left wrapped.
- **(h) Failure/retry.** Orca first; Meteora only on a *refusal* (preflight, landed-with-err, or expired-unlanded — all read off `getSignatureStatuses` with history); an unconfirmed send stops the cycle with the candidate signature preserved (the round-26 L1 repair). Every path leaves the ledger open until a return is proven: refused → `sendRejected` (closed, moved nothing, and a wrongly-refused landed sweep still pages `unledgered_sweep`); unknown → open + candidate; landed → recorded. A confirmation-timeout swap that later lands is caught on the next status read or by the chain scan.
- **(i) Extraction within tolerance.** With the 100 bps gate and 150 bps floor, an adversary controlling execution (a sandwich pushed to the gate's edge, or the thinner Meteora pool) can take at most ~150 bps of the swept value per cycle: at the ruled 10 SOL cap and 6 h cooldown that is ≤0.6 SOL/day, ~18 SOL/month — real money, entirely inside the disclosed tolerance (SPEC: "these checks do not establish a hard bound on deviation from the true market price"; holder guide: "up to 1.5% below Pyth's price, and further if Pyth's own price is off"). A stale-but-accepted oracle (≤180 s) adds market drift on top, again disclosed. The cycle size does not make it material against the revenue it protects; it is not zero and should stay in the honest list, where it is.
- **Operator key worst case:** unchanged from limit 10 — cap × windows until the guardian stops it, royalty SOL only; the key now additionally cannot sign third-party-built bytes at all. The ledger cannot read a failed cycle as closed: `cycleIsOpen` refuses on `sendRejected` only when nothing moved, and a refused row carrying a sweep is itself a CRITICAL.

4. **Get independent execution of the pinned-binary claims.** The suite proves Core is byte-identical to a pinned dump, but the pin itself was taken by this box.
