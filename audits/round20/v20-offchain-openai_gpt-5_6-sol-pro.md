# R1 — `bank/deck.mjs`

## HIGH — The committed root is not bound on-chain to the published salt commitment or slot

**Exact construct**

`commit_deck` accepts only an arbitrary `[u8; 32]` root:

```rust
pub fn commit_deck(ctx: Context<CommitDeck>, root: [u8; 32]) -> Result<()> {
    require!(c.deck_root == [0u8; 32], ...);
    require!(root != [0u8; 32], ...);
    c.deck_root = root;
}
```

Neither `sha256(bank_salt)`, slot `X`, nor `blockhash(X)` is recorded or checked. `seedFrom()` is merely an off-chain helper and is not used by the committing instruction.

**What a dishonest Bank/admin can do**

The advertised protocol is binding only if all of the following happen outside this repository:

1. Exactly one salt hash is published in an immutable, timestamped venue.
2. Exactly one future slot is named at the same time.
3. Both predate that slot.
4. The process cannot be restarted or selectively aborted.
5. The eventual salt is revealed and checked against that publication.

The code enforces none of these. A dishonest operator can:

- publish several salt commitments and later select the favorable one;
- wait for a blockhash, choose a salt and root, then falsely claim an earlier publication;
- abandon an unfavorable salt/slot and restart;
- commit a root that was never generated by `buildDeck`;
- never reveal the salt.

If a genuinely pre-existing `H(salt)` and future slot are fixed, choosing a new salt after the blockhash would require a SHA-256 preimage/second-preimage attack. That specific cheat is not practical. The defect is that the existence, uniqueness and timing of that prior commitment are not part of the certified state.

**Evidence if correct**

A local or devnet run can call `commit_deck(randomBytes(32))` without ever invoking `seedFrom`, naming a slot, publishing a salt hash, or revealing anything. The transaction will succeed and the resulting root is indistinguishable on-chain from a correctly generated one.

**Fix**

Add a precommit state machine:

- on-chain `salt_commitment`;
- on-chain future slot;
- a deadline for root commitment after the slot;
- root commitment only after the selected blockhash is available;
- on-chain or permanently archived salt reveal;
- one precommit only, with no restart path.

At minimum, produce a signed launch manifest containing the salt commitment, slot, observed blockhash, census, seed, full deck and root, and require an independent verifier to match that manifest to the on-chain root before minting is unpaused.

## HIGH — An honest deck does not prevent the Bank from choosing who receives each card

**Exact construct**

The leaf binds a level to `minted_total`, but the Bank must co-sign every mint:

```rust
require_keys_eq!(ctx.accounts.bank.key(), cfg.bank, MintError::NotTheBank);
let deck_index = u32::try_from(cfg.minted_total)?;
```

**What the Bank gains**

After the root is committed, the Bank knows the full deck. It can control recipient-to-index assignment by deciding which mint transaction it signs next.

For example, if index 417 is Level V, the Bank can:

- refuse or delay the public buyer currently waiting;
- mint index 417 to a confederate;
- consume low-level indices with its own accounts;
- reorder an off-chain queue;
- censor mints until a desired buyer reaches a favorable index.

The program prevents changing the card at an index. It does not prevent choosing the person assigned to that index. Therefore “no way to game it” and “stops the Bank handing all good cards to friends” are false.

**Evidence if correct**

Generate a known deck, advance `minted_total` to immediately before a Level V index using Bank-approved accounts, then submit two otherwise valid mint transactions in opposite orders. Whichever transaction the Bank signs and lands first receives Level V.

No hash break or proof forgery is required.

**Fix**

Commit the purchaser ordering independently of the Bank, for example:

- a publicly committed FIFO purchase queue whose entries cannot be reordered;
- a permissionless reservation instruction that binds buyer to the next index before the Bank learns or chooses the recipient;
- a batch commitment of buyer addresses followed by deterministic assignment;
- a verifiable randomness protocol where recipient and card are jointly fixed.

If the Bank intentionally retains allocation discretion, remove all claims that the deck prevents favoritism.

## MEDIUM — Salt non-reveal makes the randomness claim unverifiable indefinitely

**Exact construct**

There is no reveal instruction, deadline, bond, penalty, or fallback. Only `deck_root` is stored.

**Impact**

Proofs show that minted levels belong to the committed root. They do not show that the root came from the advertised salt/blockhash shuffle. If the Bank never reveals the salt:

- holders cannot reconstruct the complete deck;
- nobody can verify the census embedded in the tree;
- nobody can verify the advertised random seed;
- the Bank suffers no on-chain consequence.

This is not merely a cryptographic limitation. It is a protocol omission: the claim remains unfalsifiable at the Bank’s discretion.

**Evidence if correct**

Complete mints using valid proofs and then never publish the salt. Every on-chain operation continues normally. A third party can verify membership proofs but cannot derive the deck from the claimed seed.

**Fix**

Require reveal before minting, or require it by a deadline backed by a bond or automatic sale halt. A holder verifier must consume an immutable reveal artifact, not an operator-provided file that can appear after allocations are known.

## Merkle construction assessment

I did not establish a practical Merkle forgery under SHA-256:

- leaf and internal encodings are separated by `0x00` and `0x01`;
- leaves include the exact little-endian index;
- duplicate levels do not produce duplicate leaves because the index differs;
- replaying a proof at another index changes the leaf hash;
- odd-node promotion creates variable proof lengths but does not by itself enable a wrong-index proof;
- forging a different proof chain to the fixed root still requires a SHA-256 preimage or collision.

There is, however, no commitment to tree size or census beyond the root. The verifier accepts any sibling-chain length up to 24. Consequently, global properties such as “this root contains exactly 2,100 cards in the stated proportions” come entirely from the off-chain reveal process, not from `mint_vault`.

The JavaScript/Rust interoperability suite also does not prove all production shapes. It would miss a disagreement reachable only at:

- production-sized 2,100-leaf proofs;
- right-edge paths with repeated odd-node promotions;
- maximum proof length;
- a specific index not reached by the 90-card validator run;
- a different Node or Solana SHA-256 implementation/version.

A production vector set should include every distinct proof-length/promotion shape and at least the first, middle, final and maximum-depth indices of the exact 2,100-card deck.

# R2 — Level on-chain

## HIGH — Arbitrary or mismatched level caps can permanently corrupt the advertised census or halt minting

**Exact construct**

Initialization requires only:

```rust
level_caps.iter().all(|c| *c > 0)
cap_total <= max_supply
```

It does not require:

- the published 45/25/15/10/5 proportions;
- `cap_total == max_supply`;
- the committed deck census to equal `level_caps`;
- the deck length to equal `max_supply`.

The root contains no directly checkable census metadata.

**Impact**

A launch mistake or dishonest initializer can commit, for example:

- caps heavily favoring Level V;
- caps totaling less than `max_supply`;
- a deck with more cards of one level than its cap;
- a deck shorter than the advertised supply.

If a dealt level reaches its cap before the deck ends, the next mint fails with `LevelCapReached`. Because the index advances only on a successful mint and the root cannot be replaced, every later mint is permanently blocked at that same index.

This can both falsify the customer-facing odds and permanently stop issuance.

**Evidence if correct**

Initialize with caps `[2,1,1,1,1]`, then commit a deck whose first three cards are Level I. The first two I mints succeed. The third repeatedly fails at the same `minted_total`; no other level can be substituted because its proof fails, and the root cannot be changed.

Also initialize with caps summing below `max_supply`; initialization currently succeeds.

**Fix**

For a fixed product schedule:

- enforce the exact production caps on-chain, or commit a canonical schedule identifier;
- require `sum(level_caps) == max_supply`;
- commit `deck_len` and census alongside the root;
- require those values to equal the caps;
- run an exact production-deck preflight before `commit_deck`;
- monitor the committed root and caps as launch-critical state.

## Level attribute construction

I found no caller route to create the exact ASCII key `level`:

- comparison is byte-exact;
- case variants and Unicode lookalikes are separate keys and are ignored by `levelFromAttributes`;
- empty keys are refused;
- exact duplicate caller keys are refused;
- the program appends one exact `level`;
- the Core CPI with the hand-built blob is exercised against real Core.

The meaningful remaining authority is the SEAL PDA/program upgrade authority, already described in accepted limit 13.

The quota counters are transactionally safe: Solana serializes writes to the config account, and a failed CPI rolls the increments back. The defect is configuration/deck mismatch, not a concurrent counter overrun.

**Exact construct**

`scanPda` treats a syntactically valid empty DAS response as “owns no Core assets”:

const env = judgeDasEnvelope(...)

for (const it of items) {

The residual race still needs to be stated honestly; DAS cannot make this a proof.

## OPEN/BLOCKING 22 remains a real release blocker

The production path performs approximately:

- collection DAS paging;
- 2,100 sequential `fetchAsset` calls;
- 2,100 sequential ATA account reads;
- up to 2,100 sequential confirmed transfers.

A transient failure at vault 1,900 is caught as an unreadable level, but the job continues reading the remaining vaults and eventually records a whole-distribution refusal. A retry repeats the complete scan. This is expensive and likely to encounter provider rate limits or multi-hour execution time.

This is already listed as open and blocking, so it is not a new finding. The required live exercise must test:

- provider rate limiting;
- HTTP 429 and retry policy;
- a failure late in the scan;
- process restart;
- RPC inconsistency between DAS, Core account reads and ATA reads;
- total duration under realistic confirmation latency;
- exact RPC credit cost;
- lock lifetime and cron overlap.

The customer-triggerable unlevelled window is otherwise closed for currently minted assets: ordinary transfers, activation and bare burns do not rewrite the Attributes plugin.

# R4 — Packet bound

## HIGH — Test 11.11 does not measure the claimed packet limit

**Exact construct**

```ts
let maxAttrs=-1
for(let n=0;n<=7;n++){
  if(await attrFit(n)) maxAttrs=n
  else break
}
rec('11.11', ..., String(maxAttrs>=0&&maxAttrs<MAX_ATTRS_BORSH))
```

`MAX_ATTRS_BORSH` is 8, but the loop never tests 8. Therefore `maxAttrs < 8` is guaranteed whenever any tested transaction fits. It does not establish that the eighth attribute fails serialization.

Test 11.12 then calls `mkVault` with eight attributes. That transaction may serialize and be rejected by the program’s `attributes.len() < MAX_ATTRS` check. There is deliberately no `because()` assertion distinguishing packet rejection from `TooManyAttributes`.

**Impact**

The reported measurement “seven fit and the eighth does not serialize” is unsupported. Open blocker 23 is based partly on a test that cannot distinguish the packet wall from the program’s Borsh bound.

The real production tree is also not “depth 11.” For 2,100 leaves:

```text
2100 → 1050 → 525 → 263 → 132 → 66 → 33 → 17 → 9 → 5 → 3 → 2 → 1
```

That is 12 parent steps. Because odd nodes are promoted, proof length varies by index; it is not one constant “deck depth.” Some right-edge proofs are much shorter, while worst-case proofs contain 12 siblings.

Relative to a seven-sibling proof, a worst-case production proof adds five hashes, exactly 160 instruction-data bytes. The current suite never measures the exact production deck’s worst proof and real provenance strings.

**Evidence if correct**

Change `attrFit` to test `n = 8`. It is likely to serialize with the tiny `kN`/`v` attributes even though the program later refuses it. Record the serialized byte length before sending and inspect the rejection reason.

Then construct the exact 2,100-card deck and measure every proof or at least the maximum-proof indices.

**Fix**

Replace 11.11/11.12 with a pure serialization matrix that:

1. tests 0 through 8 without invoking the program;
2. records exact byte length;
3. uses the exact 2,100-card production deck;
4. finds the maximum proof length over every index;
5. uses the actual production name, URI and provenance key/value strings;
6. asserts the required product attribute set serializes at the worst index;
7. separately asserts the program rejects eight caller attributes with `TooManyAttributes`.

Do not commit the production root until this exact transaction is measured.

# Accepted limits — four requested questions

## 1. Is any accepted limit worse than stated?

Yes.

- **Limit 9 is worse than stated.** A valid but stale DAS response can omit a newly nested foreign Core asset. The co-signer does not categorically fail closed; it fails closed on transport/shape errors, not indexing lag.
- **Limit 13 remains dependent on SEAL upgrade authority**, and the owner/Bank rewrite tests do not cover that authority.
- **Limit 1a is internally inaccurate**, though in the safer direction: confinement is on-chain, while the allowlist and fee payer remain policy.

## 2. Is anything listed as accepted not actually a limit?

Yes, part of **1a**.

The Bank-pubkey confinement is already enforced on-chain by both SEAL and MINT. It must be removed from the accepted off-chain-only claim. The program allowlist and fee-payer limitations remain real.

## 3. Is any discharged item not actually discharged?

- **14:** discharged for exact duplicate caller keys. The exact reserved `level` key cannot be duplicated through `mint_vault`.
- **19:** discharged against ordinary customer action, assuming no pre-existing legacy vaults and no hostile SEAL upgrade. Activation no longer creates an unlevelled window.
- **12/17:** the measurement discharges the specific concern that the new confinement scan’s own compute overhead reaches the CU wall before the byte wall. The prose should not generalize this to arbitrary extra instructions: a byte-small instruction to another program can consume large compute, although a customer making their own mint transaction fail gains no theft or persistent denial.

## 4. Is an OPEN AND BLOCKING item being quietly treated as accepted?

Operationally, yes.

`scripts/cron-install.sh` installs `distribute.mjs` **live by default**, while limits 22 and 23 are still open and blocking. A fresh installation therefore schedules a real payer even though the production-scale live-chain read and exact production packet budget have not been completed.

**Evidence:** run the installer against a fresh crontab; the resulting distribute line has no `--dry-run`, and the script prints “LIVE FROM THE NEXT 09:07.”

**Fix:** install distribution in `--dry-run` mode by default. Require an explicit, signed launch action to remove it only after blockers 22 and 23 have attached measurement artifacts.
