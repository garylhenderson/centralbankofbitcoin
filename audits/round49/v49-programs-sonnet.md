### R2. The pool

I proved the invariant "pool balance >= ceil(owed_scaled / 1e12)" by induction over every writer of `owed_scaled` and of the pool balance:
- **Credits.** `credit` (`lib.rs:1914`) adds `delta * w` with `delta = floor(avail * S / w)`, so `owed += <= avail * S`, so `ceil(owed') <= ceil(owed) + avail <= balance`, because `avail = balance - ceil(owed)` (`distribute`, `monthly_inline`). Flooring leaves dust in the pool for the next `avail`.
- **Claims.** A vault pays `pay = floor(pending / S)`; the pool loses `pay` and `owed` loses `pay * S`; the vault keeps `carry = pending - pay*S < S` and `debt = weight * acc`, so its new pending equals the carry. `ceil(owed)` falls by exactly `pay`.
- **Retirements.** `retire` subtracts the vault's whole pending from `owed` and leaves the money in the pool (returned to the next `avail`). A redemption requires `pending < S` unless the pool is frozen (forfeit); both branches subtract exactly what they drop.
- **Joins and weight changes.** `join` sets `debt = w*acc` (pending 0). `reweigh` moves the owed share to `carry` and sets `debt = after*acc`, so `owed_scaled` is unchanged. I re-read `owed_of`: `w*acc + carry - debt` cannot underflow because every path that changes `w` resets `debt`.
- **No other mover of cbBTC out of the pool exists:** the pool is the config PDA's ATA, and the config PDA signs in exactly one place, `claim`'s transfer (`lib.rs:1727-1740`).
- **Counters.** `waiting_to_join = pending_count - pending_this_epoch`. `activate` adds to both; `advance_epoch` zeroes `pending_this_epoch`; `join` subtracts from `pending_count` only (it requires `dist_epoch > request_epoch`, so it was not in this epoch); `retire` of a pending vault subtracts from `pending_count` and from `pending_this_epoch` only when `request_epoch == dist_epoch`. All consistent. `total_active_weight` moves in `join` (+level weight), `reweigh` (+/- difference) and `retire` (- full weight incl. boost), and the 260-epoch host test `the_boost_schedule_through_the_programs_own_functions_across_four_wraps` asserts `total_active_weight`, `owed_scaled`, ring slots, `boosts_due` and `boosts_active` against a model rebuilt from records (it runs the program's own free functions).
- **Liveness.** Nobody can make a distribution impossible: a waiting vault can always be joined by a `claim` naming it (the dest equality is its recorded ATA; the account need not exist), a due boost can always be expired by `expire_boosts` with records only, and a bare-burned waiting vault can be retired by anyone. `JoinWaitingFirst` and `BoostsExpireFirst` therefore always have a free, permissionless cure. A frozen pool stops paying claims but not `retire_redeemed` (forfeit), and `monthly_inline` refuses to credit from a frozen pool. The explicit `distribute` has no such guard; the holder guide says so ("it is credited and waits").

### R2b. Boost

- **EXACTLY N.** A boost bought at epoch E for N gets `end = E + N`; it weighs from the next distribution. Distribution number N advances `dist_epoch` to `end`, and in that same `advance_epoch` the ring slot `end % 64` moves into `boosts_due`. `credit` ran with `w` that included the boost, so it earned N credits. The next `distribute`/`monthly_inline` is refused while `boosts_due > 0`, so `expire_boost` always sees the accumulator exactly where the N-th credit left it.
- **Ring.** Live not-yet-due boosts have `end` in `(dist_epoch, dist_epoch + 63]` (63 is `MAX_BOOST_EPOCHS_BOUND`, enforced at `initialize` and at `set_boost_terms`), which is 63 consecutive epochs, so no two share a slot mod 64; a slot is drained the moment its epoch is reached. `unschedule_boost` picks the same branch the advance picked (`dist_epoch >= end` means due). The host test `the_boost_ring_wraps_exactly_against_a_model` covers 1,000 epochs.
- **Weights.** `reweigh` is the only writer of `boost_weight`; `boost_weight_total` is written in `boost_vault` (+) and `unschedule_boost` (-). A vault with a boost leaves with it in `retire`.
- **Owner-only and buyer's terms:** `boost_vault` reads the asset's current owner from the account bytes and requires `burner`; `price <= max_price && extra >= min_weight && epochs >= min_epochs`. Classic SPL burn of the pinned mint only.
- No one can expire a boost early (`BoostNotDue`), twice (the second sees `boost_weight == 0`), or fail to expire it (no token account is needed).

## 5. Given that no human firm will ever see this and the programs will be immutable, what should the project do before launch that AI audit cannot?

1. **Run the real thing on the real ids, with real money, before `--final`.** The dress rehearsal used throwaway program ids and the localnet key's sibling; the programs that become permanent are different accounts. Between ceremony (step 10) and lock (step 11), keep the upgrade authority and do one full small-value pass on the production ids from a clean wallet: a charter claim of a real PoB if one can be spared (or a round if one has passed), fund a vault with 21,000 sats, request, tick, join, distribute, claim, vote on a no-op proposal, boost if enabled, and finally redeem that vault through the real page in Phantom. A bug found here is fixable; one found after step 11 is not.
2. **Decide the guardian on purpose, as a one-way door.** Gary's single hot wallet is both `init_authority` and guardian. The guardian can only stop, but a stolen key can stop sales and the sweep permanently while fewer than half the vaults vote (SPEC 47), and the value is unchangeable without that vote. Consider initializing `guardian` as a Squads vault (it can sign through a CPI; the ceremony and `emergency_stop_*` do not care) with a threshold that still lets Gary act within minutes. This is a business trade-off a model cannot make for him.
3. **Have a human reproduce the five SHA-256 values on a machine you do not control** (the GitHub verifiable-build workflow does this; confirm the run is green on the final commit and that `solana-verify` reports the same hashes against the deployed accounts after step 9), then compare the deployed ProgramData byte-for-byte with an RPC you did not use for deployment.
4. **Independent read-back of all four configs and three token accounts by a tool that is not the ceremony page** (F1's second half), signed off by a second person who did not run the ceremony, before step 11. Include the vault collection's update authority and its single royalty plugin.
5. **A legal opinion, not an audit.** Selling NFTs whose holders receive 85% of revenue in bitcoin is a securities-law question in several jurisdictions; the charter claim, the $CBOB boost and the guardian's powers all affect it. No tool in this repository can certify it, and the structure cannot be changed afterward.
6. **Real adversaries.** Put a bounty on the production ids during the charter window (only funds at stake attract the people who find ordering and economic bugs), and keep the Core watch and the alarm running from a second, differently-owned box so a compromise of this one cannot silence both.
7. **Write the contingency down now.** Immutability means the remedy for any program bug is a migration: new programs, a vote or a guardian stop on sales, and holders redeeming into whatever replaces the old ones. Pre-write who decides, what the redeem page says when sales are stopped, and what the Squads will do with rescued vaults. Also decide who holds the CBOB release wallet's key (limit 56), because it is the largest custody exposure that survives the lock.
8. **Pin and escrow the build.** F1, plus keep the exact toolchain image, `Cargo.lock` and the `dist/` bytes in two places, so the claim "this is the audited build" is checkable in ten years.
9. **A human walk-through of the holder guide with a few non-technical holders on testnet-equivalent flows.** Several promises (the 2.1% limit, the freeze, "only open on the Bank's page", add-ons off the NFT) are true but easy to misread; the damage from a misread is permanent.
