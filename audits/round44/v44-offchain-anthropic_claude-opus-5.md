## R5. THE SWAP AND THE REVENUE CYCLE

### R5‑1 (MEDIUM) `bank/swap.mjs`: the swap's liquidity‑structure accounts are derived but never checked to exist, so `market()` can report a venue "usable" that cannot execute — and it reports it *before the SOL is swept*

**The construct.** `orcaSwapIx` (`swap.mjs` 177‑196) derives three tick arrays from `orcaTickArrayStarts(w.tickCurrent, w.tickSpacing)` and passes all three unconditionally. `meteoraSwapIx` (225‑246) derives three bin arrays from `meteoraBinArrayIndexes(p.activeId)` and appends all three as remaining accounts. `market()` (419‑437) reads **six** accounts — the two pools, the two Pyth feeds, the Meteora bitmap, the Clock — and nothing else. The tick arrays and bin arrays are never read.

Both programs require those accounts to be **initialised accounts of that pool**:

* Whirlpool's `Swap` declares `tick_array_0/1/2: AccountLoader<'info, TickArray>` with `has_one = whirlpool`. Anchor validates the owner and discriminator *before the handler*, so an uninitialised array is `AccountOwnedByWrongProgram` / `AccountNotInitialized`.
* The handler then does `SwapTickSequence::new(tick_array_0.load_mut().unwrap(), tick_array_1.load_mut().ok(), tick_array_2.load_mut().ok())`. **The `.ok()` on arrays 1 and 2 exists precisely so a caller may repeat array 0 when the next arrays are uninitialised** — that is the padding pattern Orca's own SDK uses. This builder never pads.
* Meteora's `swap` (the 24‑byte, no‑`remaining_accounts_info` variant this builder emits, which `judgeSwapInstructions` pins at `d.length !== 24`) deserialises every remaining account as a `BinArray` of the pool. Bins above the active price are only initialised when someone has provided liquidity there; `i+1` and `i+2` on a ~$0.9M DLMM are not guaranteed.

Concentrated‑liquidity pools do **not** guarantee that the arrays adjacent to the active one exist: an array is initialised when a position boundary lands in it, not merely because liquidity spans it. `sim/swap-fork.mjs`'s `plan()` already encodes this knowledge — `want.filter((_, i) => infos[i])` clones only accounts that exist — which means the fork can only ever exercise the case where all three exist. SW.1/SW.4/SW.8/SW.9 passing proves they existed at that snapshot; it proves nothing about the state three months after a 15 % move in SOL/BTC, when `floor(tickCurrent / 1408)` has shifted and `s0 − 2·span` is a new address.

### R5(a) oracle math — CORRECT

`sats = lamports · sol/btc · 10^(e_s − e_b) / 10` is implemented exactly: numerator scaled when `e ≥ 0`, denominator when `e < 0`, both branches in BigInt. Rounding is floor on `expectedSats` and floor on `minSats`; both are the *permissive* direction by at most one sat, which is immaterial. `PYTH_EXPO_MIN/MAX` (−18..0) bounds `10n ** BigInt(±e)` so no exponent can make the arithmetic huge or zero. `satsPerLamport`'s `Number()` conversions carry ≤ 2⁻⁵³ relative error at any magnitude in range (round 43 already measured 1.1e‑16 worst of 100,000 draws), against a 1 bps granularity — MiMo's precision claim stays disproved. The 150 bps floor comfortably covers Orca's 16 bps fee plus the impact of a 10 SOL trade in a $10M pool; the fork measured 7.6–7.7 bps under the oracle at the ruled cap.

### R5(b) Pyth verification — SUFFICIENT, with one bound worth writing down

The address is derived (`[u16_le(0), feed_id]` under the push oracle), the account must be owned by the receiver, `verification_level` must be the `Full` tag (byte 40 == 1, which is the Borsh enum tag — `Partial` is tag 0 + a u8, so a partial update cannot be mistaken for full), the feed id must match, and the minimum length (101) is exactly right for `feed_id | price | conf | exponent | publish_time`.

No, nobody can write a chosen price there: that address is a PDA of the push‑oracle program, so only the push oracle can sign for it, it only writes Wormhole‑verified messages, and it refuses an update whose `publish_time` is not newer than the stored one, which blocks replay of an old verified message. The clock is the chain's `Clock` sysvar at the pinned address, with an owner check, read in the same `getMultipleAccountsInfo` as the prices — so age is judged at one slot against the chain, not the box.

**The residual freedom, stated as a number:** whoever posts the update chooses *which* verified message in `(stored_time, now]` to post, and we accept anything within 180 s. So the accepted price can be the most favourable valid price over a ~180 s band. Because the 100 bps deviation gate compares the *pool* against that same oracle, a manipulated‑low oracle makes an honest pool look >100 bps away and the swap is **skipped**, and a manipulated‑high oracle raises the floor and the swap is **refused on chain**. Oracle manipulation therefore mostly buys a denial of service, not a bad fill. The composed worst case for a *fill* is ≈ **250 bps below true market** (up to 100 bps of accepted oracle/pool divergence, plus the 150 bps floor). Limit 10 correctly says these checks "do not establish a hard bound"; the 250 bps composition is the number it is missing and is worth adding.

### R5(h) failure and retry — the classification is sound; one stated condition is load‑bearing

I traced web3.js 1.98's three shapes. A preflight refusal carries `"Transaction simulation failed"` / `"custom program error"` and no usable signature → `refused`. A transaction that **passed preflight and failed on chain** produces `Status: ({"err":{"InstructionError":[…,{"Custom":…}]}})`, which matches **none** of `PREFLIGHT_REFUSAL` (the status JSON says `Custom`, not `custom program error`) → it falls through to `getSignatureStatuses(…, searchTransactionHistory: true)`, which is the correct behaviour and what the selftest pins. Expired‑and‑absent → refused; settled‑with‑err → refused; settled‑ok → landed; anything else → `unknown`, cycle open.

The one place the judgement can be wrong is `expired && !status` against a lagging node: if the sweep actually landed, the row is marked `sendRejected` with `refusedSig` (never `sweepSig`), so `cycleIsOpen` is false — **but** no row claims that signature, so the MINT transaction walk pages `unledgered_sweep` CRITICAL and `detectSweepState` pages `sweep_unledgered_state` CRITICAL from account state. Fail‑loud both ways. For a **swap**, a wrongly‑judged refusal leads to a Meteora attempt; that attempt's wrap needs another `lamportsIn` of SOL, which the operator does not have *if and only if* limit 10's D2(1) rule ("the operator wallet must hold nothing but the cycle's funds and its fee float") holds. Round 43's C14 already recorded that "cannot double‑swap" is conditional on the float rule and recommended a SPEC clause; I confirm it and repeat the recommendation, because the clause is the only thing standing between a wrong classification and a double swap.

**Every path leaves the ledger open until a cbBTC return is proven on chain:** `returnedSats` is written only after a chain read of the operator's cbBTC account and a `delta >= guaranteed` check, and `cycleIsOpen` additionally requires a split. ✓

## R2. THE ALARM

### R2‑1 (LOW) A one‑pass failure to read SEAL's config silently narrows the reserve watch **and discards the $GARY / $GIRAFFISH baselines**

**The construct.** `alarm.mjs` 538: `const reserves = (cfg['seal.reserve_mints'] || [payment.toBase58()])`. When `readConfigs`' `g('seal', …)` sees a null account it sets `seal._missing = true` and produces no seal keys, so `reserves` collapses to `[cbBTC]`, `legs` is built for cbBTC only, and `balances` contains only `<vault>` keys. On a delivered pass `next.balances = balances`, so the `<vault>:<gary>` and `<vault>:<giraffish>` baselines are **deleted**. The next pass re‑establishes them at whatever the balances then are, and `detectBalanceDrops` iterates `prev` keys only — so a $GARY or $GIRAFFISH fall that happened anywhere in that window is never compared, ever.

Round 24's L5 repair (`carryConfigBaseline`) covers the **config** baseline and is correctly wired; it does not cover **balances**, and `judgeReserveRead`'s per‑element carry cannot help because the legs for those mints are never built at all.

**What is lost.** A live sealed vault's reserve can only rise (contents leave only through a redemption that burns the vault), so a drop in a live vault is, by the alarm's own words, "the drain the seal exists to prevent". That is the one case this watch exists for, and it is the case the blip can hide.

**Reachability.** Exactly the premise round 24 L5 already accepted: "a one‑pass failed read of SEAL … reads exactly that way". The pass does page CRITICAL `config_drift` for every seal key (old → null), so a human is told *something* — but not that two thirds of the balance baseline was dropped, and the drop is permanent.

**Evidence if I am right.** Stub `getAccountInfo(SEAL_CFG)` to return `null` for one pass and inspect `bank/state.json`: the `<vault>:<mint>` keys for $GARY and $GIRAFFISH are gone, while `<vault>` remains. Restore SEAL, lower a $GARY balance in the stub, and `detectBalanceDrops` reports nothing for it on the following pass.

**Fix.** Derive `reserves` (and `payment`) from the carried baseline the alarm already computes — hoist `D.carryConfigBaseline(prev?.config, cfg)` above the balance loop and read `seal.reserve_mints` from it; keep any `prev.balances` key whose mint could not be determined this pass, and raise `balance_unreadable` HIGH for it rather than dropping it. **Gate 5:** a both‑ways arm over the extracted balance block (as R39 C02 already does) with `seal._missing` set — baselines preserved, a later drop paged — shown red at this commit.

### R6‑1 (LOW) `scripts/run-tests.sh` exits **1** on a box with no DNS to the RPC host, after a run in which every gate it executed passed

The last statement in the file is

```bash
else
  echo "  SKIPPED sim/swap-fork.sh — no network. ..."
  [ "${CBOB_SHIP_GATE:-0}" = "1" ] && die "..."
fi
```

With `CBOB_SHIP_GATE` unset the `[` fails. `set -e` exempts it (it is part of an `&&` list and is not the command after the final `&&`), so the shell does not abort — but the `if` compound returns 1 and it is the script's last command, so **the script's exit status is 1**. The header says this case "SKIPS it loudly rather than failing the build"; it fails the build. The identical construct for the mainnet probe sits mid‑script, where the pending status is discarded, so only the swap‑fork one bites.

This is the fail‑safe direction (a run that skipped a gate reports failure), which is why it is LOW and not higher. The cost is that a developer on a network‑less box sees a red runner with no `FATAL` line and learns to disregard the exit code — which is the failure mode this project has repeatedly paid for elsewhere. **Fix:** `if [ "${CBOB_SHIP_GATE:-0}" = "1" ]; then die …; fi` (or append `|| true`), in both skip branches. **Evidence:** `bash -c 'set -e; echo hi; [ 1 = 2 ] && echo b'; echo $?` prints `hi` then `1`; and `./scripts/run-tests.sh` on a host with the Helius name unresolvable exits 1 with no `FATAL:` in its output.

## GIVEN THAT NO HUMAN FIRM WILL EVER SEE THIS AND THE PROGRAMS WILL BE IMMUTABLE

Five things this process structurally cannot do for itself, each with what to do instead, concretely:

**1. It cannot observe the world. Make the world a gate, on a schedule.** R5‑1 exists because the code reasons about mainnet accounts that only mainnet can answer for, and the one test that touches mainnet (the fork) clones only what already exists. Extend `scripts/probe-mainnet-facts.mjs` with a `--preflight` mode that asserts, against mainnet, every external precondition the swap and the pages depend on *at that moment*: the three Orca tick arrays and three Meteora bin arrays for the current prices exist and are owned by their programs; the bitmap extension exists and is DLMM‑owned; both Pyth sponsored accounts are Full, on‑feed and under 180 s old with confidence under 30 bps; the four third‑party upgrade authorities are unchanged from recorded values; the pool/revenue/Squads cbBTC accounts exist and are unfrozen; Core's deployed bytes equal the pin. Run it hourly from cron as a seventh job and page on failure. The one fact this round could not settle (item 22.5's DAS `burnt` flag) belongs in the same probe, measured once and then asserted forever.

**2. It cannot watch a clock. Put the clock in a gate.** The things that will break after launch are the ones that drift: a tick array that stops existing, a Pyth feed that gets deprecated, a DAS provider that changes its burnt semantics, Metaplex upgrading Core (which already happened twice under this suite and was only caught at a rehearsal with a cold cache). The Core re‑pin plus the mainnet‑probe pin check is exactly the right shape — **generalise it**: nothing external may be trusted by a hardcoded constant without a probe assertion that the constant still matches the chain, and no cache may be reused without the probe having run.

**3. It cannot tell "nobody acted" from "nothing happened". Rehearse the human loop, not just the code.** Item 22's named items cover the machine paths. Add the *people* paths and record who did them and how long they took: the guardian wallet actually pulling `emergency_stop_sweep` from a phone (timed from the first page), a person actually reading an `undeclared_token_account` report and writing an ack, a person actually clearing a `tx_skipped_unreadable` watermark after reading a signature in an explorer, a person actually returning a rescued vault from the Squads.
