## R1. The crank (`bank/crank.mjs`) — no defect found

I checked every plan against the program's refusals, specifically hunting the four things the prompt names:

- **A plan the program will refuse.** The send order (reschedule → capture → reveal → rescue → retire → preJoins → expire-before → split → distribute → expire-after → activate → claims) is the one place this could hide, and I traced the `monthly_inline` interaction explicitly. The preJoins are `claim` instructions, and `claim` runs `monthly_inline` first — but `monthly_inline` declines while `waiting_to_join() > 0`, and at every preJoin there is always at least the current claim's own waiter still unjoined when `monthly_inline` runs (the join happens *after* it in the same instruction). So no preJoin can run the month in-line and defeat the explicit distribute — the RT3-6 repair is not re-introduced by the preJoin ordering. The distribute's `w` (total + preJoin − retiring − expiring) matches the program's `total_active_weight` at execution; `owedAfterRetire` matches `retire`'s `owed_scaled -= pending`; the tick condition (`w == 0 && pending_this_epoch − retiringThisEpoch > 0`) matches the program's `NoActiveVaults`/`tick` path, including from a frozen pool (MINT's `distribute` reads the pool with `read_token`, which returns state without refusing a frozen account, then takes the `w == 0` tick branch before using the balance).
- **A plan that pays or joins a dead vault.** Burned assets are retired first and excluded from joins and claims; `'other'` (null read) assets are excluded and reported, and an unreadable eligible vault suppresses the distribution (`JoinWaitingFirst` honored).
- **A reveal out of order.** The reveal run is strictly from `revealed_total`, stops at the first not-ready, and stops at the first failure; `readySlot` matches MINT's `try_capture` stride arithmetic (verified 1000 → 1016 against `LEADER_GROUP_SLOTS=4`, `ENTROPY_GROUP_STRIDE=2`).
- **Moving value anywhere the program doesn't pin.** The crank signs only as fee payer; the selftest's allowlist arm names exactly the ten permissionless instructions and asserts no token/system transfer is built. Verified.

`budgetPlan` keeps the floor in reserve on every branch (round 30 L2), and `judgeDistributeRefusal` correctly distinguishes a race (epoch moved) from a wrong plan (epoch unmoved) from `PoolShort` (always a fault).

The sweep helper's upgrade authority and bytes are watched (`prog.sweep.*`), and `detectMissingComponents` covers it past the lock date.

**INFO — `unreadPasses`/`unreadSkips` advance on undelivered passes.** In `bank/alarm.mjs` `main()`'s `if (!delivered)` rollback block, `cursors`, `backlogs`, `sweepJudgedThrough`, `sweepCooldownSeen`, `revenueSince`, `vaults`, `frozen` and `paged` are all rolled back — but `unreadPasses` and `unreadSkips` are not (they were written into `next` before the block and are never reset). So a permanently unreadable signature accumulates its three strikes across passes whose findings were never delivered, and is skipped "on the record" without a single `tx_unreadable` page having been emailed. The skip *is* surfaced persistently once delivery resumes (`tx_skipped_unreadable` is a level finding that stands until a person writes the watermark), so the cost is bounded and arguably defensible — but it is inconsistent with the file's own stated rule ("the alarm keeps every cursor when the page is not delivered"). *Evidence if true:* `git diff` of the `if (!delivered)` block shows no `next.unreadPasses =` or `next.unreadSkips =` line; a test that fails delivery three times with one unreadable signature would show the signature in `state.unreadSkips` with zero notices sent. *Fix:* add `next.unreadPasses = prev?.unreadPasses ?? {}` and `next.unreadSkips = prev?.unreadSkips ?? []` to the rollback block, with a both-ways arm.

**INFO — a skipped signature is re-read and re-counted if the cursor is later held by a different fault.** A skipped signature lands in `unreadSkips` but not `unreadNow`, so the persisted `unreadPasses` drops it. If another signature then holds the cursor, the skipped one is re-read each pass, re-counted from 1, and re-skipped three passes later (deduped in `unreadSkips`; `tx_unreadable` is level so no new email). Wasted RPC reads only; the safe direction. *Fix (optional):* consult `unreadSkips` before re-reading.

## R5. The swap and the revenue cycle — no defect found; the judge is complete for its stated purpose

**(a) Oracle math.** `oracleFloor` computes `lamports · sol.price · 10^(e_sol − e_btc) / (btc.price · 10)` in BigInt with the exponent sign handled by multiplying numerator or denominator; both divisions floor (conservative for a floor). I recomputed the pinned vector by hand: 2 SOL at 120.45355552/85,929.10904675 (expo −8 both) → expected 280,355 sats, floor 276,149 — matches the round-40 C02 pin exactly. `satsPerLamport` is float but feeds only the deviation filter. Exponent bounds (−18..0) refuse a floor-breaking exponent.

**(b) Pyth verification.** The account is the derived sponsored-feed PDA (shard 0, push-oracle program), required to be **owned by the receiver**, `verification_level` tag must be 1 (Full), and the feed id must match. Offsets (tag at 40, feed 41..73, price 73, conf 81, expo 89, publish_time 93) are correct for `PriceUpdateV2` with a Full verification level. Ownership plus derivation is sufficient: only the receiver program can write that account, and the age is judged against the chain's Clock sysvar (never the box), with a 30 s future bound. A stale-oracle replay is bounded by the 180 s age, the 30 bps confidence, and the pool-agreement filter — the accepted residue of limit 10.

**(c) Deviation filter.** Both spot functions return sats per lamport in the right direction (verified against the fork's SW.7–SW.9, which executed 7.7/7.6 bps under the oracle on the real pools — a unit error would put both ~90,000 bps out).

**(d) Minimum out.** The same `minSats` rides Orca's `other_amount_threshold` (offset 16) and Meteora's `min_amount_out` (offset 16), in sats, the units both programs expect.

**(e) The judge.** Requires the exact seven-instruction sequence: CU limit 400,000 and no CU price anywhere; both ATA creates for the operator's own wallet and mint (full key lists); one System transfer of exactly `lamportsIn` to the operator's own wSOL; SyncNative of it; the swap with the pinned pool, the operator's wSOL in and cbBTC out at their pinned positions, amount and floor in the data, Orca's direction and price-limit bytes pinned; one close back to the operator. Unchecked swap sub-accounts (vaults, tick/bin arrays, oracle, event authority) are validated by the pool programs against the pool's stored addresses, and the on-chain floor binds the output regardless — so a tamper there can only fail the swap, never redirect it. Meteora key 9 (`host_fee_in`) is unpinned, but a host fee comes out of the pool's fee, never below `min_amount_out` — the already-stated round-42 C07 residue. The judge's same-tree limit (it cannot catch a tampered module) is limit 10's accepted box-owner residue, and the post-swap delta check (`delta < guaranteed` throws, cycle stays open) is the after-signing backstop.

**(f) Derivations.** Orca tick arrays [s0, s0−span, s0−2·span] for a_to_b (verified −88733/16 → −90112/−91520/−92928), the oracle PDA, vaults read from the pool; Meteora bin arrays [i, i+1, i+2] for Y→X, the bitmap passed as the program id when absent, `host_fee_in` as None, the event authority PDA, two classic token programs. A boundary or uninitialized array fails on-chain under the floor — loud, never a misprice.

**(g) wSOL.** A pre-existing wSOL balance returns on the close (same transaction, so no delegate can act mid-flight); a frozen account fails the cycle loud; exact-input means nothing is left wrapped but the pre-existing balance, which the close returns.

**(h) Failure and retry.** `classifySendFailure` asks the chain: preflight refusal, landed-with-error, and expired-never-landed are `refused` (Meteora may be tried); landed-and-succeeded is recorded; anything else stays `unknown` and the cycle stays open with the candidate signature preserved for hand recovery. A confirmation timeout on a swap that later lands is recorded as landed, never double-swapped. Every path leaves the ledger open until a cbBTC return is proven (`cycleIsOpen` requires `returnedSats > 0` and a split).

**(i) Adversary bound.** With a 100 bps deviation gate and a 150 bps on-chain floor, a sandwich or an edge-pushed pool can take at most the oracle-to-floor spread (~150 bps of the swap, ≈0.15 SOL on a 10 SOL cycle) and can equally only force a refusal (loud, open cycle); a stale-but-accepted oracle adds ≤180 s of SOL/BTC drift; a manipulated Meteora pool is skipped past 100 bps and floored regardless. Per month (~120 cycles at the ruled cadence) the theoretical ceiling is ~18 SOL, realistically far less — material but bounded, and it is limit 10's accepted shape. The operator key's worst case is unchanged: cap × cooldowns until the guardian stops it, never a vault, the pool, or sale cbBTC.

## GIVEN THAT NO HUMAN FIRM WILL EVER SEE THIS AND THE PROGRAMS WILL BE IMMUTABLE — what AI audit structurally cannot do, and what this project should do before launch

The suite, the coverage gate, and the freeze answer three structural weaknesses of AI audit (it reasons but never executes; it sees only the bundle; rounds never converge). They do not answer these, and no further AI round can:

1. **Run the dress rehearsal for real (items 22/53), including the parts no suite simulates.** One full live revenue cycle through *each* pool with real RPC latency and rate limits, Orca forced to refuse so Meteora actually runs, and one forced post-sweep failure walked through the hand recovery to a clean `--audit` — with signatures recorded. The crank's I/O at ~2,100 vaults (paging, a transient RPC error on vault 1,900, the every-minute reveal lane under load) has never existed anywhere but a local validator. This is the single largest remaining gap, and it is a procedure, not a read.
2. **Settle item 22.5 with one DAS read.** Whether DAS sets `burnt` on a burned Core asset is unsettled, and the alarm's entire bare-burn detection (`detectVaultSetChange`) rests on it. A "no" edits `bank/`. One read against mainnet answers it; no amount of reasoning about the code does.
3. **Prove the alert path reaches a human, end to end, in production.** The router arms in the selftest run the real router in throwaway state directories; nothing has ever proven a page reaches Gary's phone from the production cron at 03:00. Run the drill: force a harmless HIGH and watch it arrive. An alarm that has never woken anyone is a hypothesis.
4. **Have a human read the four signed `initialize` transactions against `launch/mainnet-init.json` before signing (item 38).** `check-launch-params.mjs` validates the JSON, not the transaction; the ceremony builds the transaction *from* the JSON, and the read-back-after-initialize is the only check that the bytes that landed are the bytes that were judged. The rehearsal value set's own rule — "a read-back that is not ours means close the programs and redeploy" — is a human act.
5. **Make the key-custody decisions no audit can make.** The $CBOB release wallet (limit 56, "Gary's call"), the swap operator key, the crank key, and the deploy authority are hot on one box, and the alarm and the ledger are the same user — so the detection half does not survive a compromise of the box (limit 10's stated residue). Moving the release wallet to Gary's wallet or a Squads and deleting the box key is a decision, not a finding.
6. **Publish the holder surfaces (item 50) and the holder guide, and choose the redemption-post mechanism.** The pages are staged, not published; the guide may not be published before they ship; the poster has no sender. Until a human publishes the redeem page, items 9 and 44 have no preventive control in production — only post-hoc detection. That is a launch procedure, and it is the one the holders' "crack your vault" promise actually rests on.
7. **Decide, as a person, to accept the third-party upgrade keys.** Orca, Meteora, Pyth's receiver and push oracle, and Metaplex Core are all upgradeable by keys that are not ours; the alarm detects and cannot prevent. The suite re-pins Core deliberately, which is the notification mechanism — but the decision to keep resting the vaults' seals, thaws, sweeps, burns and the swap's floor on those keys is a business judgment, and it should be re-made consciously at launch, not inherited from a checklist.

None of these is a reason to delay the audit gate's conclusion on this tree; they are the road (steps 8–11) that the gate explicitly does not cover.
