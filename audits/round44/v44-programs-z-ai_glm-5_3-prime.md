## R1 — custody: the seal, the pairing, the emptiness proof

I re-attacked the pairing and could not break it. SEAL's walk starts at `current+1` and scans forward for a top-level BURN instruction matching discriminator + asset-at-0 + owner-at-2; BURN's walk scans `[0, current)` for the mirror. Both bind asset *and* owner, so a burn borrowed from another vault's redemption in the same transaction fails (3.9/3.10/9.0/4.10). A "fake" instruction with the right discriminator and accounts *is* a real `burn_vault` call to Anchor, so it must pass all of BURN's checks to survive; a failing one reverts the thaw with itself. The sysvar walk sees only top-level instructions; the CPI-wrapper arms (WR.1–WR.4) plus `retire_redeemed`'s stack-height-and-current-instruction check close the one-level-down shapes. The account-index pins (0 and 2) match both programs' Anchor structs and are asserted in the suite (3.1c–3.1f); a future reorder fails the match and halts redemption, never leaks it.

The emptiness proof: I traced the closed-account presentation. After SPL `CloseAccount`, the account presents to later instructions in the same transaction as no-longer-Token-owned, which is why BURN's reserve loop (`if acc.owner != SPL && != T22 → continue`) skips what the sweep closes — the closed account passes *for the right reason*, and the same presentation explains SR.12a's measured refusal (`ProgramError::IllegalOwner` displays as "Provided owner is not allowed", which resolves the SR.11/SR.12a pin puzzle coherently: the duplicate source's second read hits a non-Token-owned account). A stranger cannot occupy an ATA-derived address (System allocate/`create_account` need the PDA's signature; InitializeAccount3 on dust is refused by Token — R36.G2a/b), cannot create a Token-2022 ATA for a classic mint (F4.2), and cannot set a delegate or close authority on the vault's accounts (only the owner via Execute can). A thawed vault cannot leave a transaction unburned: the burn either lands or the thaw reverts with it.

## R1s — the sweep helper

(a) *Access control.* Direct calls need the asset-signer's signature (SR.1); through Core, only the asset's owner is approved (SR.5/5a/5b/5c/5d, measured against the pinned binary, including the collection-authority and owner-added-delegate refusals and the MINT-PDA off-curve pin). It cannot be reached while frozen — the PermanentFreezeExecute rejects Execute (D5.3, 3.7) — so the helper is unreachable outside a genuine redemption.

(b) *The pair list.* Every refusal branch is measured with a pinned reason (SR.6–SR.14, R36.C5). Duplicate sources refuse (the second read of the closed account); a source that is also a destination is caught by the `signer == dest` refusal or the owner checks; a wSOL source is a plain classic account and unwraps correctly (transfer then close); a delegated source fails at Token (and only the owner can create one); a frozen source with a balance reverts the opening (SR.14) and the page refuses it before signing; a non-ATA destination is fine because the destination wallet is always the signer's choice.

(c) *Destination checks.* Safe given (a): whoever can invoke the helper is the owner or the vault's own wallet, so the destination is their choice. The rent-floor behaviour is measured (R41.C8a–c) and the canonical destination (the fee-paying owner) exists.

(d) *The lamport sweep.* `signer.lamports()` is read at execution and moved in full; nothing between the helper and the burn can deposit (only the owner's own instructions run there), and BURN's `lamports == 0` sees exactly what the sweep left. The new Core's fixed execute fee does not disturb this — the suite's exact-delta arm (SR.4a) was re-run green against the re-pinned Core.

(e) *Interaction with BURN.* Covered above: the sweep closes what it empties, and BURN skips closed accounts because no token program owns them. The prior-transaction contract (item 42) plus the permissionless ATA creation means a stranger's pre-created, pre-funded reserve account is swept to the redeemer, not stranded. I could not construct an account a stranger can create for the vault that the sweep cannot name and BURN will not skip.

(f) *Fit.* S8.0's 1,061 bytes reserves the 52+96 a real page adds; W5 measures multi-C5 shapes under the limit and the default budget; the page drops strays that do not fit and names them.

(g) *Evidence.* The arms can all fail (each `because` pin names a program-specific error, and SR.12b is a negative check on the recorded error). The helper's suite-deployed bytes are the bytes build-mainnet proves: the sweep has no `localnet` feature, so the localnet build, `target/deploy` and `dist/` are one build, and build-mainnet `cmp`s all three.

## R2 / R2b — the pool and the boost

I re-derived the invariant "the pool always holds at least ceil(owed)" under `credit`, `claim`, `join`, `retire` (both forfeit and non-forfeit), `retire_burned` and `reweigh`: `delta·w ≤ avail·SCALE` in credit; the claim's settlement leaves `balance − pay ≥ ceil(owed − pay·SCALE)` exactly; retire only decreases owed. The counters (`pending_count`/`pending_this_epoch`, `active_count`, `total_active_weight`) conserve under every ordering I tried, including the F21 shapes (waiting vault redeemed and bare-burned in the same epoch) and the K.7/BO.32/BO.36 overlap shapes (crank and pairs naming one record). Liveness: every gate (`JoinWaitingFirst`, `BoostsExpireFirst`, `PoolShort`) has a permissionless exit that the crank exercises; a griefer can only add waiting vaults by funding real vaults, and anyone can join them.

The boost: a boost earns exactly its N distributions (the distribution that reaches `end` makes it due; nothing distributes past a due boost; expiry is free and permissionless); `reweigh` preserves the owed share exactly through carry; the ring arithmetic is self-cleaning (every `advance_epoch` consumes its slot, ends are ≤ 63 epochs ahead so a slot is never consumed early, and early retirement decrements the right slot — the wrapped-ring retire is BO.38/BO.38a); no record can be expired twice (weight is zeroed), early (the gate skips it) or never (the crank and any claim expire it). The host-side model tests over 260 epochs and four wraps are the strongest single piece of evidence in the tree.

## GIVEN THAT NO HUMAN FIRM WILL EVER SEE THIS AND THE PROGRAMS WILL BE IMMUTABLE, what should this project do before launch that AI audit structurally cannot?

1. **Run the dress rehearsal with a human's hands on every step, and treat it as the certification it is.** Item 22's named items — one full live revenue cycle through each pool, one forced post-sweep failure walked through the hand recovery, the crank against a live cluster, the alarm in rehearsal mode — are things no reader can do. A real Phantom prompt, a real Squads return, a real RPC under load, and the feeling of a recovery runbook at 2 a.m. are not auditable from source. Record the signatures; the item already demands it.
2. **Rebuild the release bytes on a second machine and compare the sha256s.** `build-mainnet.sh` is honest that its key proof rests on reproducibility ("the bridge is reproducibility: rebuild from $COMMIT and confirm these sha256 values"). One independent rebuild, by a different person on different hardware, closes the one gap the box cannot close about itself.
3. **Move the hot keys with a witness before the charter window opens.** The $CBOB release wallet (limit 56), the swap operator key, and the deploy authority should be moved to hardware or a multisig with a second person present and the move recorded. AI audit can read that the key is mode 600; it cannot audit custody.
