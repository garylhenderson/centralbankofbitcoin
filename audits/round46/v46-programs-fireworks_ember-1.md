## R1. Custody: the pairing holds

I attacked the pairing in every shape I could construct. SEAL's forward walk and BURN's backward walk both bind asset (account 0) and owner (account 2), both read top-level instructions only, and both fail closed. Concretely: `[open A(O), transfer A→O2, burn A(O2)]` fails because SEAL finds no burn for (A,O); the same with the burn naming O fails BURN's live owner read (NotTheOwner). `[open A, burn A, burn A]` dies on the tombstone's length. A wrapper fails the sysvar check in `retire_redeemed` (current instruction is the wrapper, not `burn_vault`) and the stack-height check is height-2-only, so a direct call (height 1) and a double-wrapped one (height 3) both refuse — WR.1-WR.3 measure all three. There is no re-seal instruction anywhere, and the plugin authority is SEAL's PDA, so a thawed vault cannot be re-frozen by the owner and cannot leave a transaction unburned: the burn lands or the thaw reverts. The rate-limit ring is a true rolling window — `kth = ring[(head + 64 - k) % 64]`, refuse unless `now - kth >= window` — and I verified the cap arithmetic: `ceil(live*210/10000)`, floor 1, `k = min(cap, 64)` (stricter past live 3,047, never looser; 45/hour at 2,100).

## R1s. The sweep helper

(a) **Access:** directly, it needs the asset-signer's signature, which only Core gives, only to the asset's owner, only while thawed (SR.5/5a/5c/5d measure each). ## R2. The pool invariant holds

I re-derived it rather than trusting P.32. `credit` adds `delta = floor(avail*SCALE/w)` to `acc_scaled` and `delta*w ≤ avail*SCALE` to `owed_scaled`, so `ceil(owed)` grows by at most the undistributed balance. `claim` pays whole sats and subtracts exactly `pay*SCALE` from `owed_scaled`, preserving `pool ≥ ceil(owed)`. `retire` (redeemed, forfeited, or bare-burned) only ever *decreases* `owed_scaled` while the pool is unchanged. `join` sets `debt = w*acc`; `reweigh` moves `owed_of` into `carry` and restarts debt at the new weight, leaving `owed_of` and `owed_scaled` untouched. `owed_of` cannot underflow because debt is only ever set to `weight*acc` or `weight*acc` in full. The counters (`pending_count`, `pending_this_epoch`, `active_count`, `total_active_weight`) survive every ordering I could construct, including request-then-advance-then-retire (the same-epoch guard) and request-request-advance-join-join. Liveness: `JoinWaitingFirst` is clearable by anyone for free (join needs no token account), `BoostsExpireFirst` is clearable by `expire_boosts` (records only), and a redemption itself unschedules a due boost. No one can make distributions or redemptions impossible for others.

## R2b. The boost earns exactly N

A boost bought at epoch E with end = E+N earns the distributions advancing E→E+1 … E+N-1→E+N — exactly N — and becomes due at E+N, gating the next distribution until expiry. The ring cannot collide: live end epochs span at most 63 distinct values (max_boost_epochs ≤ 63 = BOOST_RING-1), and slot (E-1)%64 is provably empty when a boost ending at E+63 is written to it, because reaching epoch E required the boost that slot held to have become due and been cleared first. `unschedule_boost`'s due/not-due branches both decrement the right counter; `retire` unschedules from wherever the boost sits. The host test drives 260 epochs (four wraps) through the program's own functions against a model rebuilt from the records; the suite's BO.* arms measure the orderings (boost+distribute, distribute+boost, due-retire, frozen-defer expiry).

## What this project should do before launch that AI audit structurally cannot

1. **Run the dress rehearsal with a human watching the money, not just the record.** Item 22's named items — one full live revenue cycle through *each* pool and one forced post-sweep failure walked through the hand recovery — are precisely what no suite, sim or fork executes. A person should watch the first real sweep's cbBTC return land and reconcile it by hand before the loop is trusted.
2. **Two people verify the deploy and the lock on an independent machine.** The release gate proves source == RELEASE_COMMIT; it cannot prove the five deployed ProgramData accounts hash to `dist/`'s sha256 with zero tails, nor that the five `--final` transactions landed. Read the ids from a printed card, not from the box's own output.
3. **Read the initialize values aloud before signing.** The checker validates the JSON, not the signed transaction (round 24 A6), and the values are permanent: charter dates, guardian, swap operator, treasury, the three reserve mints, the PoB root — each read against `launch/mainnet-init.json` by a second person at the ceremony.
4. **One real charter vault through the published redeem page with real money before any announcement** — the only surface no simulation covers is the published page with a real Phantom.
5. **The hot keys' custody is Gary's decision, and AI can only state it.** The $CBOB release wallet (56), the swap operator (10), the PoB collection authority through the charter window (46) and the guardian (47) are bounded and disclosed; whether the operational custody matches the disclosures is not something any audit round can decide.
6. **A human on the alarm for the first 48 hours** — the first real distribution, redemption and reschedule deserve a person reading the pages, not only the detectors reading the chain.
